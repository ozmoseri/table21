# TABLE21 competitor scan: two mobile blackjack apps

Scope: public sources only (store listings, distributor pages, press). No app was installed, decompiled or logged into, and no assets were extracted. All links accessed 2026-11-02.

Selection:
1. **Blackjack 21: Blackjackist** (KamaGames): social, real-time multiplayer, free-to-play chips.
2. **Blackjack** by Tripledot Studios: single-player, offline-capable, free-to-play chips with ads.

Together they bracket TABLE21: one is the "social table" model, the other the "fast casual solo" model.

## Cross-app observations
- Both apps publicly position "free chips, no real money", guest/no-registration start and a learn mode. [Play listings]
- "Rigged dealer" complaints appear for both, one social and one solo, so they are not caused by multiplayer. Both developers answer with "certified RNG" text, which does not let a player see anything for themselves.
- Install sizes of 307 MB and 442 MB (App Store) are far above what a PWA needs.

## Implications for TABLE21
Each item is tied to a design decision. Nothing here suggests copying art, sounds, characters, code or text from either app.

1. **Round result summary (fairness you can see):** after settlement show a per-hand summary with the player's and dealer's final totals, the outcome word (win, push, bust, blackjack) and the coin delta in half-coin-correct display. Also keep a short last-N-rounds history from server snapshots. This answers the "rigged" theme with visible facts rather than a claim. Never expose the shoe or unrevealed cards.
2. **Table rules strip:** a small always-available rules line on the table: deck count, dealer stands/hits soft 17, blackjack payout, and a "shuffle" marker when the server reshuffles. This covers the "show the deck count" request without leaking shoe contents.
3. **Lobby order:** instant play first (solo or pass-and-play, no account wall), then "Table with friends" (create or join by code), then wallet/daily grant and profile. Tutorial stays optional and skippable, never a forced gate.
4. **Social scope:** friends-only rooms with preset reactions instead of open free-text chat. This avoids the etiquette and moderation problem reported for public tables, and fits a closed friend group.
5. **No interruptions mid-hand:** no pop-ups during a hand. The daily coin grant and any notices appear only in the lobby or between rounds (same safe-point rule as updates). TABLE21 has no ads or purchases, so keep it that way.
6. **Dealing pace and weight:** own pace budget with a fast-deal setting and tap-to-skip (see techniques.md), plus a small first-load payload as a deliberate advantage over 300 to 440 MB native installs.

## What could not be verified
- **No gameplay footage was found** through search for either app (YouTube searches returned only store pages). As a result, table layout, seat count, dealing speed, skip controls, win/payout presentation and lobby screen order are **unverified** for both apps.
- Store screenshots were not readable as text through the fetch tool, so no visual claims are made.
- App Store "last updated" dates show day and month without year (Blackjackist "Sep 25", Tripledot "19 Aug"). Google Play dates (Oct 29, 2026 and Oct 26, 2026) are the reliable activity signal.
- Blackjackist's developer naming differs by store (KamaGames on Play, Wise Wave Corporation Limited on App Store). The relationship was not investigated.
- Review points come from the few reviews each store page shows by default. They are qualitative signals, not a sample, and do not measure prevalence.
- Features listed by the stores (tournaments, daily bonuses, albums) are developer claims and were not tested.
